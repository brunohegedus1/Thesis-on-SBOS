\chapter*{Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Summary}

In 2000, Background Oriented schlieren proposed to replce the large optical elements used by traditional schliern with a cross-crorrelation algorithm \cite{Dalziel2000}. The cost was a sensitivity that inherently tied to the physical distances in the optical setup, leading to a constant compromise between sensitivity and resoluion \cite{Raffel2015, Schmidt2025}. Replacing the printed backgroung pattern with a laser-speckled field expand that constraint \cite{Meier&Roesgen2013}. Since the speckle is always in-focus, it can be positionally anywhere in the line of sight of the camera, allowing for unusual focus positions, not only behind the test section but also in-front of it and inside it. The resulting technique, Laser-Speckled Background Oriented Schlieren, remains relatively unexplored compared to standard BOS and lacks a coherent design framework for its implementation. This thesis investigates the application of the method to a supersonic wind tunnel, through four experimental campaigns that address each of the three components of the method: the optical configuration, the speckle generator, the
refractive object and finalizing it with the unique implementation to a high-speed wind tunnel.

A design methodology was first developed to solve the optical setup performance parameters for a set of physical variables, to correctly size the experimental setups. That allowed the identification of the variables that influence the sensitivity-resolution ratio and allows for improved performance of SBOS face BOS. It also gave rise to surface maps that allowed the main trends of the design space to be identified and transformed into coherent design guidelines using a Spatial resolution Criterion. An angled glass slide that induced an known light displacement served to validate the methodology and compare different in-focus positions. A dedicated campaign on the speckle pattern established that the f-stop can be used to control the average speckle size while the optical
magnification does not, which contradicts the Rayleigh established theory. Nevertheless, it lead to a generator capable of producing optimal speckles sizing between 2 to 5 pixels.An under-expanded compressible jet was then imaged and its displacement field converted into a quantitative density field through an Abel inversion with Tikhonov regularization. The results agree with the shock cell correlations of Emden \cite{Emden1899} and of Hartmann and Lazarus \cite{Hartmann1941} and, over the first shock cell, with the centerline density of Panda and Seasholtz.

The final experimental campaign implementated the method to image a linear aerospike nozzle flow at freestream Mach number equal to 2, in TU Delft's ST-15 supersonic wind tunnel. With the focus inside the test section the usual model $S = l_{eff} M_{ag}$ no longer holds, so a method was introduced that recovers the sensitivity from the ratio of displacements measured at two configurations, which agreed within 5\%. In-focus SBOS presented similar spatial resolution to schlieren images of the same flow, capable of identifying the same compression waves, expansion fans and shear layer. This work proposes a design route for SBOS setups and shows that the method can be applied in a high-speed wind tunnel with a simple optical arrangement and a
low powered laser.
