Laser-Speckled Background Oriented Schlieren (SBOS) is an optical density measurement technique that replaces the printed background of traditional Background Oriented Schlieren (BOS) with a laser speckle pattern. This background has higher contrast, is tunable through the optics, and is always in focus, allowing unusual reference placements. This thesis develops a design framework and tests it in four experimental campaigns. The methodology showed that placing the background in front of the test section improves the sensitivity-resolution ratio over standard BOS. Tests with an angled glass plate across multiple optical setups confirmed the predicted sensitivity gains. Laser speckle generation was also briefly studied to produce speckle sizes of 2--5 px and ensure optimal cross-correlation. An under-expanded jet was imaged using the method. It yielded a quantitative density field that was compared to literature data, matching the first shock cell. Finally, SBOS was implemented to image a linear aerospike nozzle in a high-speed wind tunnel. With the focus inside the test section, the results approached similar resolution to schlieren, and a new method was used to retrieve the sensitivity at zero defocus. By developing, validating and implementing a design framework for SBOS, this work lays the groundwork for future SBOS implementations.
