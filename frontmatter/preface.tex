%Quote

The quote above always reminds me that there are more things beyond my knowledge than I can immediately see or could ever conceive. Since this work set out to see the invisible changes inside transparent media, it seemed only fitting to start with it. The methods that this work is built upon are a remarkable example of human ingenuity and striving to see further than the visible. Having learned something about the pioneers of schlieren imaging, I can only express admiration and respect for their work. To take mundane observations, describe them, understand them, make predictions, test them, implement the result and then improve it: this is the core of scientific discovery, and it can be seen throughout their work. With this thesis, I have tried to make a small contribution to this field based on these principles, by building on the work of those who came before me, examining it, organizing it, trying to improve it and adding something new. I hope this work helps those who come after me to expand our understanding of the world further, remembering that there are always more things between heaven and earth than are dreamt of in our philosophy.

Of course, I couldn't have done this work alone. I received a lot of support throughout this thesis, and for that I am forever grateful. I would like to express my gratitude towards my supervisors, Alessio Secchiaroli and Ferry Schrijer, for the guidance, feedback and insights that they provided over the past months. I would like to thank all the staff and technicians who helped me adapt to the laboratory procedures and operated the wind tunnels during the experiments. I also want to thank the committee for accepting the invitation to attend my MSc defense. On a more personal note, I want to thank all my friends who cheered me on throughout this journey, be it in presence or in thought from afar. Finally, I want to thank my parents, who ultimately made this achievement possible and never left my side, even across the ocean. Thank you all.
