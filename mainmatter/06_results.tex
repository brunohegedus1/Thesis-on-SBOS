\section{Angled Glass Experiment Results}

The Angled Glass Experiment aimed to validate the methodology and compare the different defocus configurations treated in chapter \ref{chapter: methodology} . This section will display the data that has been collected, present how it was treated and discuss if these objectives have been achieved. the tables that follow give an overview of the results.

Table \ref{tab: angled glass displacement results} compares the displacements measured through SBOS with the predicted shift induced by the angled glass. $\Delta_x$ is the mean of the cross-correlation vector field in pixels, $\Delta x_{SBOS}$ is the latter transferred to mm through $\rho_{px}$ and $\Delta x_{glass}$ is the glass-induced ray shift, calculated through equation \ref{equation: Displacement of angled glass}. Since the glass piece produces an uniform displacement field, the standard deviation of the cross-correlation field $\sigma_\Delta$ quantifies the precision of the SBOS measurement, while the relative error quantifies the accuracy of the method. All errors are below 10\%, with most of them below 4\% and $\sigma_\Delta$ also shows small values. The only outlier was point 1 with a relative error of 7.87 \%. Given that the optical magnification and $d_f$ error shown in Table \ref{tab: df and Mag angled glass results} do not standout for point 1 compared to other points, most probably the source of the error was in the measurement of the glass angle for that point. Nevertheless, these metrics indicate that the technique has been well implemented and is capable of producing accurate and precise results.

Through Table \ref{tab: df and Mag angled glass results}, the design strategy of chapter \ref{chapter: methodology} can be assessed by comparing the optical distances and magnifications it prescribes with the ones obtained through the in-focus plane calibration. The Table is restricted to the 9 points where a similar field of view was enforced and the methodology of chapter \ref{chapter: methodology} used to size the optical setup. The relative magnification errors are contained mostly below 5\% with point 7 as an outlier. Point 7 was obtained using the minimum distance which the calibration could be in focus when using an $f=105$mm lens. That distance was bigger than the one prescribed by the surface map, resulting in a large $d_f$ and $Mag$ error. The propagation of that error is also evident in the sensitivity showed in Table \ref{tab: sensitivity comparison}.

Through the data collected during the experimental campaign, it is possible to obtain sensitivity through two routes and each give a different insight on the error sources and validity of the experiment. Table \ref{tab: sensitivity comparison} compares the theoretical sensitivity given by the surface map of chapter \ref{chapter: methodology} to sensitivity obtained through the geometry $S_{geom} = l \cdot Mag_{exp}$ and the sensitivity obtained with the displacement data $S_\Delta = \Delta x_{SBOS}/\epsilon_{glass}$. With the exemption of point 7, which has already been treated, all data is within 8\% of agreement. Strangely point 2 and 9 show a larger error in this table compared to the previous ones. This larger deviation might come to a measurement error in the lever arm $l$ which was used to compute $\epsilon_{glass}$. The data also validates the methodology of chapter \ref{chapter: methodology} as it demonstrates that the physical setup reproduces the predicted performance.
