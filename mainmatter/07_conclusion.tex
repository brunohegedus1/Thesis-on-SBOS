\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chapter: Conclusion}

This thesis investigated the implementation of Laser-Speckled Background Oriented Schlieren in a supersonic wind tunnel. The method was approached through four experimental campaigns of increasing complexity that tackle each of its three main components: the optical configuration, the speckle generator and the refractive object. First, Chapter \ref{chapter: methodology} presented a design methodology developed to balance the sensitivity and spatial resolution of a SBOS setup to reach an optimal physical layout. This methodology was applied, tested and refined to design the experimental setups described in Chapter \ref{chapter: Experimental Setups}. Different optical configurations were tested to image the distortions produced by a simple refractive object, which allowed for the validation of the methodology and comparison of the various setups' performance parameters. A dedicated campaign investigated the production of speckle patterns, arriving at a configuration capable of producing speckles with average diameters between 2--5 px. Then, an under-expanded compressible jet served as a test subject for the quantitative density reconstruction of a real schlieren object using displacement fields obtained through SBOS. The final experimental campaign used the method to study the flow of a linear aerospike nozzle in a high-speed wind tunnel environment. The results gathered and discussed in Chapter \ref{chapter: Results} make it possible to answer the main research question and the five sub-questions posed at the start of this thesis in Chapter \ref{chapter: introduction}.

\section{Practical guidelines for the design of a SBOS setup}
\label{section: Research question 1}

The first research sub-question intended to discover what should be the practical guidelines for designing a SBOS setup. Chapter \ref{chapter: methodology} partially answered this question by summarizing the design guidelines resulting from the analysis of the design space surface maps. These recommendations survived the implementation in real experimental setups. However, further recommendations were collected throughout the duration of the thesis and can now be presented as a whole to aid the implementation of this technique by other researchers.

Regarding the \textbf{optical setup}: 

\begin{itemize}
    \item A negative defocus configuration is preferable to positive defocus. It provides a higher magnification for the same defocus distance, this way improving the Sensitivity-Resolution trade-off presented in Equation \ref{equation: S over smin}.

    \item A higher $f_\#$ is preferable as it allows to easily achieve a better sensitivity-resolution trade-off. However, the smaller aperture might reduce contrast and require larger exposure times, which increase motion blur. SBOS uses a laser directly pointed at the camera, which produces high luminous contrast, allowing for very small exposure times.

    \item A longer focal length tends to make the setup more robust. A longer $f$ widens the spacing between isolines in the design space, so the performance parameters become less sensitive to positioning errors during assembly. It also raises the sensitivity ceiling of a positive defocus setup, given by Equation \ref{equation: BOS Sensitivity limit}. A moderately shorter lens can be adopted when the physical size of the setup is constrained, at little cost in sensitivity and spatial resolution.

    \item The SR criterion can be used to match the optical and processing resolutions. Equation \ref{equation: SR} allows to select how many $s_{min}$ could be imaged for a certain field of view. Equation \ref{equation: smin px} also expresses the minimum distinguishable feature in pixels, which allows the interrogation window to be selected so neither the optics nor the software resolution limits the measurements.

    \item The use a commercially available compound camera lens lead to a shift in the prescribed distances prescribed by the methodology. The optical magnification should, therefore,  be taken from a calibration target at the in-focus plane rather than from the design values.
\end{itemize}

Regarding the \textbf{speckle generator:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item A low power laser is sufficient for SBOS measurements. The 0.5 mW class 2 continuous laser used in this work produced a pattern of adequate contrast and speckle pattern. The high luminous contrast also permitted short exposure times.

    \item The diffuser and the beam should be sized to fill the field of view. The diffuser should be large enough to cover the desired object field of view, and the beam should be expanded to illuminate it. A beam that is too narrow becomes diffraction limited at the diffuser rather than at the lens pupil, which enlarges the speckle and removes its dependence on the aperture. This was probably the cause of the oversized speckles of the first campaign, where a diffuser of 1.27 cm was used with an unexpanded beam, as it was discussed in Section \ref{subsection: Speckle Beam Diameter}.

    \item Ideally the speckle size should be stay between 2--5 pixels. This value comes from the literature review of Chapter \ref{chapter: LiteratureReview} and is the optimal size to avoid peak locking and not degrading the 3 dot Gaussian fit. Nevertheless, a larger speckle size can be used, given that a corresponding larger interrogation window is used, thus degrading the spatial resolution.

    \item The f-stop can be used to tune the speckle size. Section \ref{subsection: Speckle Aperture and Magnification} showed that measured speckle size followed the aperture as expected, while the optical magnification sweep produced almost no change. With a compound camera lens the aperture appears to be the only reliable handle on the speckle size.

    \item Either Projected and directed speckles can be used. Both methods produced patterns of comparable size and deviation, so the choice depends on practical considerations. Projected speckles suit applications with a single optical access or a laser powerful enough to damage the sensor, while directed speckles provide more intensity.

    \item The speckle generator should be isolated from vibrations. Any movement of the diffuser displaces the whole pattern, which introduces a shift that may lead to decorrelation.
\end{itemize}

Regarding the \textbf{refractive object:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item the strength of the density gradients can lead to excessive blur that must be counteracted with a smaller defocus. In a flow with strong gradients and considerable turbulence, the defocused configurations blurred the speckle pattern to the point of decorrelation. Consequently, the resulting displacement fields were dominated by noise, as shown in Figure \ref{fig: aerospike displacement points 1-3}. Moving the in-focus plane closer to the test section or even inside of it, proved effective to reduce the blur and be capable to image the flow.
\end{itemize}


\section{SBOS compared to BOS and Schlieren}
\label{section: Research question 2}

This work sought to answer how SBOS compares to traditional BOS. The main difference is the ability to dynamically modify the reference position by adjusting the in-focus distance. The design methodology already noticed the advantage, when it was identified that negative defocus provided a better sensitivity-resolution ratio than positive defocus. The angled glass experiment validated this claim by comparing multiple optical configurations. Later, different defocus configurations were also tested during the high-speed wind tunnel experiment, which also allowed the method to be compared with traditional schlieren. Together, these experiments allow to answer the second research sub-question and see how the performance of SBOS setups fare against traditional techniques.

The design methodology and angled glass experiment identified the mechanism that makes negative defocus preferable to the standard BOS setup. SBOS provides lower focal distances, leading to higher accessible magnification. The magnification increases the sensitivity-resolution ratio presented in Equation \ref{equation: S over smin}, meaning that a higher resolution is possible with little loss in sensitivity. Moreover, not using a physical background allows for the reference to be positioned much closer to the test section and even inside the test section, which was tested in the final experiment.

The wind tunnel experiment imaged the flow at a fixed camera position, varying the in-focus position to be in-front, inside and behind the test section. The negative defocus presented a higher sensitivity than positive defocus, with the inside position presenting the lowest sensitivity. Both defocus configurations presented spurious blur that made no structures distinguishable in the flow. The position inside the test section showed increased spatial resolution, which allowed the qualitative study of the flow. This demonstrates another advantage of SBOS: the capability to dynamically change the defocus position to increase the resolution or sensitivity on demand.

Traditional schlieren was also compared to the results obtained with SBOS during the wind-tunnel experiment. In-focus SBOS provided excellent spatial resolution, which allowed the same qualitative analysis as the schlieren images. It can be said then that in-focus SBOS has a comparable resolution to schlieren. Additionally, SBOS poses certain advantages over schlieren. The setup is considerably simpler, needing fewer and smaller elements than schlieren. Furthermore, the displacement field obtained can more easily be turned into a quantitative density field than schlieren.

The evidence collected during this thesis shows how SBOS compares to traditional techniques. Its unique capability of dynamically changing the in-focus plane allows it to reach resolutions comparable to schlieren and increase the sensitivity beyond what's possible for BOS. Moreover, its high luminous background allows for smaller exposure times, making it suitable for high-speed flows. The method's increased resolution and sensitivity also permits the imaging of small fields of view that would be unfeasible through standard BOS. The next section will treat on the main uncertainties and limitations faced by the method.
